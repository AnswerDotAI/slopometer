The spaCy rules: sentences, clauses, passives, and the patterns anchored to them

This module uses spaCy to detect long sentences, stacked clauses, passives, and part-of-speech-dependent vocabulary. It also checks noun clusters, nominalizations, actor placement, and phrases whose meaning depends on their position in a sentence. get_nlp downloads and loads the spaCy model.

from fastcore.test import *

Getting the model

Slopometer uses spaCy’s 40MB en_core_web_md pipeline. The md and lg English models share a vocabulary of 684,830 keys and the same tagger and parser architecture. The md model maps that vocabulary to 20,000 shared vectors. The 560MB lg model keeps 342,918 vectors.

The rules use the parse and vocabulary membership, including is_oov in the variation rule. They do not compare vector similarities. The para notebook explains why the proposed similarity-based detectors failed. A future rule that uses similarity would need its own model comparison.

Explosion publishes the model as a wheel on GitHub releases, outside PyPI. Slopometer downloads it separately rather than declaring a direct-URL package dependency. pip install slopometer requires no extra installation command.

model_path first checks for an installed model package. Otherwise, it downloads the model to the user’s XDG cache directory on first use. The cached copy serves projects across virtual environments and survives environment syncs.

Reading a parse

The rules use these spaCy token attributes:

  • pos_ is the part of speech, such as NOUN, VERB, ADJ, or ADV.
  • lemma_ is the dictionary form. It lets a rule match both “leverages” and “leveraged” with one entry.
  • dep_ and head describe the token’s grammatical relation to another token. For example, nsubj marks a subject and dobj an object.

The parser also finds sentence boundaries. Splitting on periods would mishandle abbreviations such as “e.g.” and version strings such as “v3.8.0”.

spaCy 101 introduces these concepts. The labels relate to Universal Dependencies, although spaCy’s English models use ClearNLP labels. Chapters 17-18 of Speech and Language Processing explain statistical parsing. Parse errors can cause both missed findings and false positives.

doc = get_nlp()('The gateway never kills an unresponsive kernel.')

Sentence load

ASD-STE100 limits descriptive sentences to 25 words and procedural sentences to 20. find_sent_len uses 25 words, excluding punctuation. It adds one weight point per extra word when the sentence also contains at least two clauses. A single-clause enumeration can exceed the cap without a finding.

_n_clauses starts at one and counts these additions:

  • Each subordinate clause labelled advcl or ccomp.
  • Each coordinated verb with its own subject. “The kernel gains ports and clients see restarting” counts as two clauses. “Reads stdin, writes to the terminal, sees the same directory” does not add clauses.

Relative clauses such as “a small program that starts fast” do not increase the count. find_clauses flags counts of four or more, with one weight point per clause beyond three.

Passive voice

“The file is deleted” omits who deletes it. find_passive flags subjects labelled nsubjpass. It assigns a lower weight when the verb has an agent child, which names the actor in a “by” phrase.

The assertion below checks spaCy’s labels. A model change that renamed nsubjpass would otherwise disable this rule without an error.

Vocabulary judged by the parse

Some words are inappropriate only in particular uses. “The impact was small” uses a noun. “This impacts performance” uses the verb where “affects” would do. “The fix landed” leaves the reader to infer whether it merged, committed, or released.

find_verb_banned checks part of speech and lemma. It excludes tokens after a hyphen, such as the end of “aviation-shaped”.

find_intensifiers flags “simply”, “merely”, and “effortlessly” as adverbs. It excludes “just” because the same part-of-speech tag covers both minimization (“it just works”) and time (“just released”).

Noun clusters and nominalizations

In “kernel connection state model update”, readers must work out which noun modifies which. find_noun_cluster flags runs containing at least three common nouns and increases the weight for each extra noun. Proper nouns do not count toward the threshold. “New York City subway” passes.

A nominalization expresses an action as a noun: “make a decision” instead of “decide”. find_nominalization checks for a light verb with a derived noun as its object. It suggests a verb from a fixed mapping, such as “assess” for “perform an assessment”.

Actor placement

Joseph Williams’s Style: Toward Clarity and Grace recommends making the doer the subject and the action the verb. Tells 22 and 23 cover two ways prose can obscure the doer:

  • Artifact-as-agent credits a document or change with its author’s action: “this PR introduces”.
  • Recipient-as-subject describes what the beneficiary receives: “the kernel gains fresh ports via the new process”.

find_artifact_agent requires an authorship artifact as subject and a verb from its crediting list. “The parser rejects malformed input” passes because the parser performs that operation.

find_recipient_subject flags “gain” and “receive” when they have a subject. It flags “get” only with a “via” or “through” phrase.

Sentence-anchored patterns

Position matters for some phrases. “This section describes” announces content at the start of a sentence, but “the part this section describes” has another purpose. sent_rule can restrict a phrase list to sentence starts.

The phrase rules cover throat-clearing, today’s-world openers, announce-then-deliver, not-X-but-Y, teasers, and appraisal preambles. find_rhetorical separately flags sentences ending in a question mark.

The not-X-but-Y rule limits its matches to patterns such as “isn’t just a”. It leaves contrasts such as “not thread-safe, but lockable” alone because they can state useful constraints.
